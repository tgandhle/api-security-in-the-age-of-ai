# Independent review of the course

This directory is how the course gets reviewed by people other than its author, and how those reviews are recorded. It exists for criterion 2 of [QUALITY.md](../QUALITY.md): every lesson covered by at least one review from a human subject-matter expert who is independent of the author.

## What you are asked to do

Pick one packet in `packets/`. Each covers the lessons for one area of expertise, so nobody needs to review the whole course:

| Packet | Area | Lessons |
|---|---|---|
| [A](packets/packet-a-identity.md) | Identity, credentials and tokens | 13 |
| [B](packets/packet-b-core.md) | Core API and web application security | 9 |
| [C](packets/packet-c-integration.md) | Integration and protocols | 5 |
| [D](packets/packet-d-ai.md) | AI, agents, MCP and A2A | 8 |
| [E](packets/packet-e-assurance.md) | Assurance, testing and operations | 3 |

You may review a whole packet or only some of its lessons. For each lesson you review, read the lesson page, then work through what the packet lists for it:

1. **Claims attributed to a source (Standard).** Does the cited source say this, at the strength stated? The course writes "requires" or "must" only where the source says MUST, SHALL or REQUIRED, "recommends" or "should" for SHOULD or RECOMMENDED, and "allows" or "may" for MAY or OPTIONAL.
2. **The project's defaults (Baseline) and alternatives (Option).** Are they technically sound? These are the project's position, so the question is whether they are right, not whether a source says them.
3. **The lab.** Run it (`python3 labs/<name>.py`; Python 3 only, except five labs that need `cryptography` and say so). Does it demonstrate what the lesson says it demonstrates? Is anything it shows wrong?
4. **The checklist items.** Is each title and detail technically correct?
5. **Anything else on the page** that is wrong, misleading, out of date, or missing in a way that would lead a reader to build something insecure.

## What you are not asked to do

You are reviewing **technical correctness**. You are not asked to approve:

- the severity rubric, or the severity given to any checklist item;
- the applicability model, or any item's applicability condition;
- the course's structure, teaching approach or writing style.

These are shown for context. If you choose to review severities or applicability conditions as well, say so in your record, and your findings on them are recorded separately from your technical review. A record that does not opt in is not evidence for or against them.

## Who counts as independent

- You did not write, edit or commission any part of the lessons you review.
- You state your relevant role or experience, and any relationship with the author.
- A review by an AI model does not count, including when a person submits it. You may use tools as you normally would, but the judgements must be yours.

## Dispositions

Every finding, and every lesson as a whole, gets exactly one of these:

| Disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Correct | Reviewed, and technically correct as written. |
| Correction required | Wrong, or the source does not say it, or says it at a different strength. Must change. |
| Clarification suggested | Not wrong, but could be misread in a way that matters. The author decides. |
| Out of reviewer scope | You did not review it, or it is outside your expertise. Not evidence either way. |

A lesson counts as covered only when its record lists which claims you reviewed. Claims you do not mention are not assumed correct.

## How to submit

Copy [RECORD-TEMPLATE.md](RECORD-TEMPLATE.md), fill it in, and post it in a GitHub issue titled "Review record: packet X". If you prefer not to post publicly, say so in a "Review offer" issue and the author will arrange another way. Do not report a security vulnerability this way; follow [SECURITY.md](../SECURITY.md).

The author then:

1. commits your record unchanged to `log/`, named `YYYY-MM-DD-<your-name>.md`;
2. answers every finding marked "Correction required" or "Clarification suggested" in the same file, under "Author's disposition": what was changed, in which commit, or why not;
3. counts the lesson as covered in `QUALITY.md` only once every "Correction required" finding on it is resolved.

Your record is published with your name and role. Only submit if you are content with that.

## For the maintainer

The packets and `manifest.json` are generated by `tools/build_review_packets.py` from the lesson pages and the checklist. Never edit them by hand. Claim ids such as `05-S3` are positions on the page at a given commit, which is why every record names the commit its packet came from.
